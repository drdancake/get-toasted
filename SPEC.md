# GET TOASTED
### A fryer-station arcade game for STL Toasted on the Hill

Working title. Alternates: *Stay Toasted*, *T-Rav Rush*, *Fryer*. Slogan is "Get Toasted, Stay Toasted", so the title is probably already decided.

Dan Drake (code) and Taj (art, 3D). Started 2026-11-03 in a cafe. We are playing. If we can't stop thinking about it, we win.

## 0. Direction v2: the lobby cabinet (Dan, 2026-11-06)

This section wins wherever the rest of the spec disagrees.

**Who and where.** A standalone arcade game anyone would love, dialed in until it feels satisfying and inviting. The real target is a touchscreen cabinet in the Toasted lobby: walk-up strangers, two-to-three minute runs, a local high-score board with initials. Playable on a phone too, always: the phone is how we playtest. If it delights strangers, it delights Matt. That's the goal.

**The feel.** A rhythm of hands. Tickets set the tempo; the score rewards flow (no dead time, no fumbles), not just results. Seconds-long WarioWare-grade microgames, strung together by Overcooked-style planning.

**The best moments on the real line** (build these first and juice them hardest): dropping the ravs and the sizzle; the blunted click of the timer button; tossing in the salt; placing each rav for a stellar presentation; the finishing touches; sealing the box and sending it off.

**The skill.** Efficient and quick-thinking: staying on top of what's in the oil and what dressings you'll need. Every system has to make that juggle richer or it doesn't go in.

### The recipe
An order is a recipe, like Overcooked: **rav type + sauce**, in a box. Classic and three cheese usually get marinara, buffalo gets ranch, but the ticket says what it says: the sauce is part of the fulfillment, not a fixed pairing.

### Sauce, cups and the guest's name (Dan, 2026-11-07)
- **The ladle game** (tap the marinara or ranch pan at the table): top thumb dips the ladle in the pan to scoop and pulls it down to tip it out; the stream wobbles, and swinging the ladle throws it. Bottom thumb slides the clear cup under the stream. Stop at the line and the lid snaps on: SAUCED! on the line, a flash if it's light or full; past the brim it runs down the side and the cup's tossed. Sauce on the table is Matt's money. Ranch pours thicker and slower. The fryers keep cooking the whole time.
- **Cup stock:** lidded cups wait on the table (each pan shows its count). Dropping one in a box uses it; at zero the pan nags and you ladle more. The lemon berry mascarpone comes pre-portioned.
- **Two cup sizes:** small clear cups for a 6 (and a 1 gooey); the big black cups for a 10 and for 3 gooey. A split 6 (beef/buffalo) gets one cup of each sauce.
- **The guest's name is the human element:** it's on the ticket, in Sharpie on the lid, and in the big moments: "CHET TIPPED BIG!" for a fast perfect ticket, "CHET HATES YOU NOW" when a box comes back.

- **Hot vs cold sauce (Dan, 2026-11-07): the skill curve.** Cold sauces (ranch, the lemon berry mascarpone; Caesar later with salads) can be made ahead: you start a shift with 12 small cups of each, as on the line, they run out, and you prep more in the slack. Marinara is hot: you start with none, ladle it as you need it, and a cup left out goes cold (a complaint, and no sauce point) and then gets tossed. The marinara pan steams when it's got hot cups; a ❄ counts the cold ones.
- **The mascarpone cup** is its own two-step game: the spatula (drag a dollop from the paste pan, swipe it off into the cup, to the line), then the squeeze bottle (hold to squeeze the berry compote, swirl it all the way around: SAUCED! for the right amount all the way round).

- **The freezer, from above (Dan, 2026-11-07).** The default freezer looks straight down. Tap a bin and a rav flies up at your face, getting bigger, tumbling in 3D, and falls back; its shadow on the floor shows where (and, by how tight it is, when) it'll land. Slide a basket under the shadows; each thumb drives its own basket. ALL 6!, NAILED IT and the count pops carry over. The original side view is one button away (SIDE VIEW in the freezer bar, or ?freezer=side) while we compare.

- **Freezer audit (Dan, 2026-11-08).** The bins carry label-maker tape on top (BEEF, CHEESE, BUFF, GOOEY); a NEED bar over the floor shows what the tickets want, counting down as you catch (0/6 BEEF, 0/3 GOOEY); the floor is darker so the white wire baskets pop, and each basket throws a shadow for height; rav shadows are tinted with a ring in their flavor's color, so you can see what's coming down; the hint is one line. About 13% harder: each volley lands somewhere new, with more spread and a slightly tighter rim (measured with a shadow-following bot: 70% caught before, 61% after).

If you can see a basket, that is the basket, and it's the same drawing wherever you take it; nothing snaps, swaps or is carried for you. The classic top-down fryers stay behind a switch on the title screen until the new stage wins.
- **Rhythm decides what stays (Dan, 2026-11-10).** The big words are beats, and the target feeling is a rhythm game's flow. A step earns its place if it's a good beat: one clear gesture, one satisfying hit, different from the last. Kept: catch, drop, pull, pour, salt, toss, fill the cup, Sharpie the name, arrange, pinch, close. Cut: wax paper (boxes come lined) and choosing a box size (tap the one stack of boxes and the next order's box is set out, the right size). Ranch is thin and fast, marinara medium and chunky, mascarpone a slow scrape; the pour is a stand-in until TJ's sauce physics is ready. The hint line is gone on the stage; alerts belong up top where the eyes are (rail, timers, fryers), the bottom half is for hands. Basket in the right hand, bowl in the left. Next: PERFECT timing on each beat, a streak, and a kitchen pulse.
- **The freezer has a temperature (Dan, 2026-11-11).** A dial on the chest reads it. It climbs while the lid is up and recovers while it's shut, so repeated long visits add up. It beeps from 32 °F and costs reputation from 41 °F, the bottom of the food-safety danger zone (41 to 135). A fryer oil temperature that dips when a lot of frozen ravs go in is an idea, not built.
- **No name is sloppy, not fatal (Dan, 2026-11-11).** A box with no readable name, sent while other tickets are up, always looks a little silly (small reputation hit). One time in four it goes home with the wrong guest: bigger hit, and a REMAKE ticket for that box jumps the pile to the front of the rail, on the house and short on patience.
- **Fewer labels, fewer screens (Dan, 2026-11-11).** A piece that needs a label is probably the wrong piece. The tongs have none: pick them up off the table and they are in your hand; pass the tips over the bowl or a box and they pinch one rav; let go over a box or the bowl to set it down. The timers and the rail stay full size at the plating line and the Sharpie. Spilled sauce is charged as it lands, a cent at a time ($1 a cup), with a tally running up on the table. The mascarpone cup is made the way it is on the line: a quarter pan in the cold table with its small spatula (wooden handle, blue blade) and a squeeze bottle of berry compote. Drag the spatula through the pan to load it (a good scoop covers the blade), put it in the cup and scrape it off against the edge, then squeeze compote into the space that's left: half and half, to the line. Too much on the spatula ends up on the rim; too much compote runs over (and onto the spill tally). One cup size for everything. Dessert sauces are seasonal, so this is one of several to come. Open: whether the parm, parsley and sugar pans should sit on the main table.
- **The freezer never gates the fryers (Dan, 2026-11-11).** With the freezer open you can still pull a basket that's done; the open lid only costs temperature. Baskets left on their bars catch ravs that fly into them (the top empty one of each stack, or one already hanging with frozen ravs), so a single launch can land without a hand on a basket; a fast volley still needs you moving one. Overfilling a sauce cup loses only what runs over: the cup is kept as a full one.
- **Sauces are a station, not a button (Dan, 2026-11-11).** The pans are on the table where they are in the kitchen: ranch and mascarpone in the cold table left of the fryers, marinara in the hot table on the right. You pick the pan; nothing tells you which sauce the next box wants (read the ticket, mind the SUBs), so the wrong sauce in a box is a mistake you can make, and it costs at the window. The cup you fill is for the box sitting next to it in the close-up, name on it, and goes into that box when it's lidded. Sauce is stuff, not a meter: it sits in the ladle, runs over the lip when you lower and tip it, piles up in the cup, and what misses is on the table and on the tally. One hand: dip, carry, lower to pour, lift to stop. Each sauce has its own feel: ranch thin and quick, marinara thick and chunky, mascarpone a scrape and a squeeze. Sauce is cupped per order (no stocking up; in the real kitchen ranch and mascarpone are pre-cupped, marinara is poured to order). No timing on hot marinara. Difficulty is Tetris-shaped: it scales until a fail state, still to be decided (the restaurant's rating is the candidate).
- **Labels belong to the object (Dan, 2026-11-11).** When something needs a name, it gets one the way a kitchen does it: the sauce pans are seen from the front and a little above, with a day-dot label stuck on the side, slightly crooked, saying what's in it and the date it was made (today). The back wall is red brick. Tap the spike to read back every box that went out, was sent back, or walked: each with everything a guest could complain about, so a penalty is never a mystery. A bobbing arrow shows where a pour goes (the bowl, or the box). Tongs take the odd one out (the gooey in the buffalo), from the bowl, a box or a basket on its hook, and can set it down in another basket. The ladle keeps its physics but calmer: finer sauce that can't fly, a ladle that loads fast and follows the hand, and a level in the cup.
- **The ladle, the way it's held (Dan, 2026-11-11).** The sauce close-up is seen the way the cook sees it: the pan at the back, the cup toward you, the box it's for beside it. The ladle is the real one: a long straight handle held at the top (the crook), the bowl hanging below the hand, one full bowl to one cup. It works like a claw machine: your thumb moves the hand from wherever the thumb is, so nothing is ever under it, and the bowl's shadow shows where it is. Over the pan it dips and fills. Brought to the cup it settles at the cup's far edge. Push away from you and the bowl swings over, sauce running over the lip toward you into the cup; pull back to stop. Stopping exactly on the line is the sensation to nail: the line lights when you're on it. The cup is in your other hand: drag it nearer the pan for a shorter carry. A full ladle moved too fast slops. Ranch is thin and stops when you stop; marinara is slow to start, glugs, and keeps coming a moment after you pull back. About a second a cup when it's going well. This replaced the side-on particle version, which was amusing and wrong. Then (same day) it became a real 3D scene with shadows, and the stroke became one motion, back to front: run the bowl through the pan (it fills on the way through), pull it toward you, it stops at the cup's far edge, keep pulling and your hand comes on toward you while the bowl swings over and pours; ease back to stop. No reversing direction to start the pour. The handle leans out to the right so the hand is beside the bowl, never over it.
- **Sauce station, round two (Dan, 2026-11-11).** A pan glows while a box on the table is waiting on what's in it. The marinara lives in a small steam table standing on the counter (stainless box on feet, dial, pilot light, steam), not sunk into it; the ranch stays in the cold well. A cup can come back out of a box: drag it to another box, or back to its pan (nothing wasted); let go anywhere else and it stays put. A tap on the box is still the box. The freezer warms a little slower.
- **The look (Dan, 2026-11-11).** Stylized and silly on purpose: the references are Elite Beat Agents, Wii Fit and WarioWare. The food is the one realistic thing (Taj models it to match the real ravs, later mozzarella sticks; TJ the sauces), and the contrast is the joke. Everything else is simple, chunky and friendly. Always daytime: Toasted is open 11 to 3, so the sun is out. The palette is the place's: Italian-flag red, green and white, black and white checkerboard, on warm wood and cream instead of steel gray. It doesn't have to look like the real room; it has to feel like part of the place. UI pieces are stickers: white or cream, a dark edge, a solid drop shadow. Matt's moods are drawn faces (six slots, furious to delighted); the current ones are stand-ins for Wella's paintings. The cabinet is a bright touchscreen about 15 inches wide, portrait.
- **Shutting the freezer (Dan, 2026-11-11).** You can shut it with ravs still in the air: the lid comes down, the freezer's front is back at once, and the baskets (in your hand or on their bars) stay out to catch what's flying, for up to a couple of seconds. Only a touch right after a bin tap is ignored as a slip. The ticket spike stands on a little wooden shelf.
- **Every step has an indicator (Dan, 2026-11-11).** Whatever is next should be asking for it on screen, to keep people moving: a box with no name has a Sharpie bobbing over its lid; a pan glows when a box wants it; an arrow shows where a pour goes. The freezer's bins are bigger and the lid's touch area starts well clear of them, so reaching for a bin never shuts it. No painted sunlight on the wall (it read as fake). The table is edge-grain butcher block: narrow strips of short staggered blocks, not floorboards.
- **Cross it out (Dan, 2026-11-11).** Scrawl a name nobody can read and you get one second chance: draw a line through it and write it again, nearly as big, underneath (the scrawl is shoved up out of the way and is only ink: the second try alone is judged). The crossed-out try stays on the lid. It saves the box from going to the wrong guest, and costs a little off that ticket's tip (`crossedOutTip`).
- **Tickets read like the real ones (Dan, 2026-11-10).** A cook scans every ticket on the rail and tallies flavors, so a ticket shows everything at once and nothing else: the name, one line per item (a 10 beef and a 6 beef are two lines, never merged), a modifier in red under its line (SUB RANCH; 86 SALT comes next). No progress bars. Patience runs along the top edge, by the rail. A Sharpie dot goes by a line once its ravs are dropped in the fryer; what's boxed is not tracked. Orders run to 6 items. A receipt is one width, always: a long order hangs longer, never wider. So the timers sit above the rail and the tickets hang down over the wall, with room to be long; touches pass through the paper. The tickets are paper in the scene, not HUD on top of it: a rav in the air passes in front of them. When the rail is full, new tickets wait face-down in a pile at its left end and are hung as room opens; their patience starts when they are hung.
- **Flow, not anxiety (Dan, 2026-11-10).** The target feeling is flow, right on the edge of anxiety and not over it. Two tickets deep was already panic, so tickets come slower (see SHIFT_TICKETS). Grind down friction that isn't fair. The freezer hand-off: nothing is carried in or out for you. The freezer opens, the baskets stay on their bars, and you grab one straight off its bar to catch with; let go and it flies back to that bar and hangs there with what you caught (grab it again to top it up). Your own hand is the connection between that basket, that bar and that flavor. The fryer baskets look like the catch basket (cool stainless, visible mesh).
- **Pouring and plating (Dan, 2026-11-09).**
  - *Pour, don't drop.* Bring the bowl (or, for gooey, the basket) to a box on the table and pull down to tip it: ravs come out one at a time, faster the further you tip, and letting go stops it. Stop right on the count for ON THE COUNT. That's how one bowl of 11 beef becomes a 6 and the beef half of a 5/5.
  - *Never mix flavors in the salt bowl.* It's allowed (they look alike, so it's a crisis, not a rule), and the pour gives you whatever's at the lip.
  - *Plating is a snap grid.* A 6 is tic-tac-toe: house style is 3 / 2 / 1 nested left with the cup bottom right. A split (3/3 or 5/5) is a column per flavor with the two cups down the middle. 3 gooey in a medium box are quadrants (top two, bottom left, cup bottom right); a 1 sits beside its cup. Any tidy layout scores; the house layout is a bonus (HOUSE STYLE, one more presentation point, so a bigger tip). Each flavor has to be one connected group so the guest knows which is which.
  - *Parm and parsley are pinched by hand from quarter pans,* not shaken. One big pinch covers everything in front of you: it falls as you sweep (more the faster you go), so one zigzag dresses the batch. Every rav needs some of each; a dot over each rav lights up when it has enough. Salt and powdered sugar stay shakers. Gooey: DUST, FLIP each one (tap), DUST again.
  - *The line (big orders).* Pour first: the close-up waits until the bowl's empty, so you can fill box after box. Then the full boxes come up one at a time, big: ARRANGE, PINCH, CLOSE IT. The shut box slides off left and the next one slides in from the right (boxes from the same ticket first), so a whole order goes out as arrange-season-close, arrange-season-close. Tap off to put the line down; what you've set stays set ("set · tap to finish"), and tapping any box with ravs in it starts the line there.
  - *Every step gets a big WarioWare word* (ARRANGE! PINCH! DUST! FLIP! CLOSE IT! POUR! SALT! TOSS! INTO THE BOX!), and the first time each move comes up in a shift, a cartoon hand shows how.

### How they connect (the freezer rule)
The freezer already works because **what you made shows up where you expect it**: the full basket is on the bar when you walk back. Every station follows that rule:
1. **Everything you make is a thing that travels.** Basket, bowl, cup, box: you see it leave one station and arrive at the next.
2. **The ticket is the map.** Recipe icons with checkmarks as each part gets done (rav, cooked, salted, sauce, boxed, labeled).
3. **The next job glows.** From the line, the station that needs you lights up, with a count of what's waiting there.
4. **Doors are fast and readable.** Scene switching stays, but every switch is a quick slide in the direction of the station, carrying what you're holding.

### Learning it
- **Attract mode:** with nobody touching it, a ghost hand plays a perfect ticket.
- **Learn to play:** a first shift where Matt walks you through one ticket, one station at a time, with a ghost hand showing each gesture. Stations unlock one by one: fryer, then freezer, salt, assembly, sauce, label.
- A new player understands the flow from watching for ten seconds, or it's not done.

### Screens
Portrait everywhere: a phone for playtesting, a big portrait touchscreen for the cabinet. Layouts scale with the screen's size and proportions (tall phone, squarer tablet); every build gets checked at both. Landscape only has to not break.

### The cabinet (later, but the code allows for it now)
- The hardware: a large, high-definition **multi-touch** screen (10-point capacitive, not IR, because the game uses more than one finger), probably a touch monitor plus a small PC, in a kiosk mode that boots straight into the game.
- **Coin gate for a cause, switchable.** Built in: with the gate on, a run costs a credit and the start screen says where every coin goes (staff set the cause). With it off, free play. Staff hold the top-left corner of the start screen for three seconds to open the operator panel: gate on/off, the cause, a test credit, a coin count. It remembers.
- The coin hardware is a cheap weekend build: a coin acceptor sends a pulse, a small USB board turns it into the arcade coin key (5) or a gamepad button, and the game counts a credit. Coins also solve hogging: a run costs something, and it goes somewhere good.
- Maybe later: high-score prizes (top score of the month eats free). Check the local rules before charging money for a game with prizes.

## 1. The one sentence

You run the fryer station at STL Toasted during an endless rush that only gets busier, and Matt is watching.

## 2. Pillars (check every decision against these)

1. **Touch first, any screen.** Phone portrait for playtesting, a big landscape touchscreen for the lobby cabinet (see section 0). Runs in a browser with no install.
2. **Grounded in the real station.** Three fryers, two basket slots each, baskets stack, the third fryer runs hotter for wings. Harder means busier, not weirder physics.
3. **Arcade, not story.** Endless, high score, faster and busier the longer you survive. Flappy Bird / Tetris / Donkey Kong lineage. Roguelite upgrade picks between rushes.
4. **Food commercial look.** Nour's lesson: saturated, glossy, tactile, cranked past ten. Fast and cute beats high resolution.
5. **Competitive and a little subversive.** 420 energy in the copy, not the mechanics. Matt pops up to say "Wow, great work!" when you ruin a basket.
6. **Build the STL Toasted brand.** The real menu, the real flavors, the real sauces, The Hill. Ends on "visit us."

## 3. The loop

One run = one **shift**. A shift is a sequence of **rushes** that never ends until you're sent home.

```
ticket arrives on the rail
  -> tap a flavor at the freezer to load a basket
  -> tap an open fryer slot to drop it (timer starts, doneness ramp begins)
  -> tap the basket to pull it (early = cold, in the window = perfect, late = burnt / popped)
  -> at the pass: tap the right shaker (parm+parsley+salt, or powdered sugar)
  -> tap the right sauce cup (marinara / ranch / lemon berry mascarpone)
  -> tap the ticket to serve
score, tips, combo
  -> ticket timers run the whole time; expired tickets cost patience
  -> every ~60-90 s the rush ends, 8 s lull, pick 1 of 3 upgrades
  -> next rush is busier: more tickets, more flavors, bigger quantities
game over: Matt's patience hits zero ("Clock out.")
```

**The whole UI for the first build is the doneness ramp.** Pale cream, to beige, to amber gold, to deep brown, to black. No bars, no numbers. Gold is good. You learn it in one basket.

## 6. Scoring

- Each rav served: perfect 100, ok 50, rejected 0.
- Ticket served before its timer: tip bonus scaled by remaining time.
- Combo: consecutive perfect tickets multiply tips (x2, x3, x4, cap x5). Any rejection resets.
- **Patience** (lives): starts full. Expired ticket -1, rejected basket -1, cornstarch incident -2. Perfect ticket at high combo heals a little. Zero = "Clock out." Shown as Matt's face getting less amused.
- High score saved locally. Initials entry, three letters, arcade style.

## 7. Difficulty (grounded in restaurant reality)

Harder is just busier. Rush N increases some mix of:
- tickets per minute
- quantity per ticket (more 10s, fewer 6s)
- flavor variety on the rail at once
- multi-line tickets (6 Beef + 3 Gooey on one ticket, both must be at the pass together)
- fryer 3 going hot (phase 2 wings mean you need it hot, so ravs in it get risky)
- then wings: breading step, longer cook, their own tickets
- then chicken parm cutlets: fry and hand off to the sandwich person, no plating, no sauce, a different rhythm

Never: random physics chaos, screen shaking, mystery mechanics. Players should always be able to say "I lost because I put the cheese in the hot fryer."

## 11. Platform and tech

- **Engine: Three.js (plain, no React), Vite, TypeScript.** Real-time 3D so Taj's models are the actual game objects, one low-poly rav mesh per flavor with a **doneness shader**: a single 0..1 uniform blends pale -> gold -> burnt albedo and darkens the fluted edges first. One model covers the whole ramp. Pop is a morph target or a mesh swap. Finish (parm/sugar) is a second texture layer with a 0..1 coverage uniform.
- Fallback if 3D is too slow on phones: render Taj's models to sprite sheets from Blender (doneness frames) and draw them in 2D. Decide after testing on a real phone, not before.
- Assets: glTF/GLB out of Blender, Draco compressed, textures 512 or 1024 max, power of two. Git LFS for `.blend`, `.glb`, `.png`.
- Audio: Web Audio via Howler.
- PWA: manifest + service worker so it installs to the home screen, runs full-screen portrait, and works offline. This is also what makes the **arcade cabinet** possible: a tablet in kiosk mode running the same build with a local leaderboard.
- Hosting: GitHub Pages or Cloudflare Pages, free. The STL Toasted site is a SpotHopper template, so it gets a link or an iframe. QR code on the counter.
- Leaderboard: local (localStorage, initials) in MVP. Global later via a tiny backend (Supabase or a Cloudflare Worker) only if people are actually playing.

## 13c. The shift: station plus microgames (2026-11-05)

Dan's second playtest: the toss is the fun part, but one screen with everything on it is clutter. Raw ravs could bounce straight into the box, and it wasn't clear how cooked ravs got out of a basket. The real station, from the line:

- **Station (home screen):** three fryers, two slots each, baskets long-ways with handles toward you. Fryers 1 and 2 at 300°F, fryer 3 at 350°F (the wing fryer: 1.3x heat, half the window). Eight baskets on the line.
- **Freezer microgame:** tap FREEZER, see the bags (OG Beef, Three Cheese, Buffalo, Gooey Butter), tap a bag, a rav hops out, catch it in your basket. Never mix flavors: cooked ravs look identical, so a mixed basket can't be served and its timer is wrong. NEXT BASKET stages the full one and hands you a clean one. DONE goes back to the fryers. The fryers keep cooking while you're in there.
- **Drop, nest, wiggle, time:** drag a staged basket into a slot. Drop a second basket onto a full slot to nest it: it holds the bottom one under (fully submerged) and comes out first. Ravs float unless you wiggle the basket side to side or nest one on top; floaters cook uneven and cap at "ok". Tap a basket's chip to start its timer.
- **Real timings, one scale:** three cheese 3:45, buffalo 4:00, gooey butter 4:45, beef 4:50, all divided by `COOK_SCALE` (16) so the order and gaps stay true. Mozz sticks (5:45) and wings come later. Gooey butter ravs are about 1.6x the size.
- **Pass microgame:** drag a basket up out of the oil and you're at the pass holding it, with a box labeled for the matching ticket. Flick the ravs in, swipe the box up to serve. The basket goes back on the rack when it's empty.

## 13d. Direction: a WarioWare collage over an Overcooked clock (2026-11-05)

The game is a loop of short, sharp microgames, each one a real step on the line, stitched together while two clocks run in the background: the fryer timers and the customers' patience. A wrong order costs more than points: it wrecks your flow (Overcooked). Like WarioWare, each microgame has a small set of tricks it can throw at you, and getting good means recognizing them instantly.

Shape of a microgame: a one-word prompt flashes ("SUGAR!"), you do the motion, and sometimes it breaks mid-motion and you fix it fast ("CLOGGED! tap it") before going back.

Catalog from the line, in order of the real process (built so far: freezer catch, fryer drop/nest/wiggle/timer, flick into the box):

| Microgame | The motion | What can go wrong |
|---|---|---|
| Freezer | tap a bag, catch in a basket | mixed flavors, drops, overfill |
| Fryer | drop, nest, wiggle, start timer, pull | floaters, forgotten timer, burnt, hot fryer |
| Wax paper | lay a checkerboard sheet in the box before the ravs | forget it, crooked sheet |
| Salt bowl | dump the basket into the silver bowl, toss to salt | spill over the rim, under-salted |
| Into the box | dump the bowl into the box on the wax paper | miss, overfill, wrong box |
| Dress | parm + parsley (savory) or powdered sugar (gooey), shake hard | shaker clogs mid-shake, wrong shaker |
| Sauce | cup in the corner: marinara, ranch, lemon berry mascarpone | wrong sauce, spilled cup |
| Close | fold the lid, press the two locking tabs | tab won't catch |
| Name | Sharpie the customer's name on the lid | (flavor, maybe a speed bonus) |
| Wings (later) | walk to the back fridge, season, cornstarch, dress, drop in the 350°F fryer, shake a lot so they don't stick | sticking, long trip |

Real details to keep: the box is a styrofoam clamshell with angled sides (a wide hexagon in profile) that seals in the middle with two locking tabs. The wax paper has a black and white checkerboard (about 1 inch checks), cut from 12 inch sheets into 6 inch squares that match the box floor.

Discipline: add one microgame at a time, play ten runs, keep it only if the loop is better.

## 13e. The full order, from the line (Dan, 2026-11-05)

This is the target loop. Microgames: **freezer** (gather), **fryer** (cook), **expo** (plate). The rail and the timers run under all of them.

**The ticket.** White receipt paper on the rail. The real ticket has sandwiches and chips too; this game only reads the fried-food lines at the bottom. A ticket can carry several items at once, and two tickets landing together play like one big combination order.

**Order sizes (real menu rules).**
- Savory ravs (beef, three cheese, buffalo): 6 or 10. Each can be one flavor or **two flavors split evenly**: a 10 of beef/buffalo is 5 + 5, a 6 of cheese/beef is 3 + 3.
- Gooey butter (dessert): 1 or 3. Never split.
- Example ticket: a 10 split beef/buffalo, a 6 of three cheese, a 1 gooey butter.

**Telling ravs apart.** Same dough outside, different filling inside, so the color difference is slight but real: beef pale tan, buffalo a little orange, three cheese whiter. Cooked, it's harder, but three cheese shows little bits of cheese oozing from the perforations (and sometimes bursts). A keen eye can catch a stray rav in any state: raw, cooked or dressed.

**A stray rav is not a ruined basket.** If a buffalo lands in the beef basket, you look in, find it, and pick it out. The game tracks every rav physically, so a stray that makes it into a box is only judged at the end, and penalties are graded:
- 5 beef + 1 buffalo in a 6 beef order: a small penalty (it's one rav).
- 6 beef sent to a buffalo order: a real failure (wrong order).
- Scoring rewards flow: multipliers for clean streaks, penalties scaled by how wrong.

**Freezer (gather).** Pick the baskets you're filling on the station first (tap the empty ones on the bars), then go in with all of them. In the freezer each basket is its own object, and each finger can steer a different one: launch beef and buffalo together and catch them in two baskets. You can leave a basket in the freezer by accident, and it's still there next time ("where the hell is my other basket?"). For a really big order, Dan would carry the whole bin out to the fryers; maybe later.

**Fryer (cook).** One fryer can carry that whole example ticket: left slot 5 beef with 5 buffalo nested on top, right slot 1 gooey with 6 cheese on top (longer cook goes underneath, comes out last). Drop them as close together as you can, then press the timers. The timers are only a guide: the game counts real seconds submerged, so a late press means you could overcook.

**Expo (plate).** Its own microgame, at the cold table:
- **Boxes in three sizes.** Small is for a 1 gooey. Medium is for a 3 gooey or a 6 of anything (a bigger order fits in a pinch but looks worse). Large is for a 10.
- **Wax paper.** Line each box with a checkerboard sheet: 6 inch squares for medium and large, 2 inch for small.
- **Label.** Mark each box for its order. Dan writes the name and contents in Sharpie on the lid; in the game this is a quick tap-label that keeps the player sane when several orders are going.
- **Salt bowl.** Cooked ravs go from the basket into a silver bowl, get salted and jostled, then go into the box.
- **Arrange (style points).**
  - A 6 in the medium box is a tetromino: three across the back, two down the left, one in the middle, sauce in the bottom right corner.
  - A 10 in the large box: four across the back, four down the left, three nested in the corner, sauce bottom right.
  - A 5/5 split: a column of each flavor down the left and right edges, both sauce cups in the middle.
  - Dumping them in is allowed, it just doesn't score style.
- **Sauces.**
  - Marinara for beef and three cheese, ranch for buffalo, lemon berry mascarpone for gooey butter.
  - Small clear cup for a 6 or a split side, large black ramekin for a 10. Both get lids.
  - Marinara is ladled from a well on the other side of the fryers. Small ranch cups are pre-made each morning; a large ranch is filled to order.
- **The cold table.** A shoulder-width lift lid over a cold table with eight clear quarter pans, 2 deep by 4 across. Back middle: ranch and Caesar. Front middle: pre-made cups of ranch and Caesar. Far right front: powdered Parmesan. Far right back: chopped parsley. A white plastic cutting board strip runs between you and the pans.
- **Dress.** A handful of Parmesan over everything, then a handful of parsley.
- **Gooey butter** gets powdered sugar, gets flipped, gets sugared on the other side, then goes in with the mascarpone.
- **Close** the lid on the two tabs and send it.
- Salads (Caesar) are also the fryer person's job. Maybe later.

**Build order from here:**
1. Real tickets: multi-item, split orders, graded flavor penalties.
2. Multi-basket freezer: select on the station, steer each basket with its own finger, pick out strays, leave baskets behind.
3. Subtle filling tints.
4. The expo microgame, in the order above.

## 13f. Scoring: Matt's money and the restaurant's reputation (2026-11-06)

Two meters, both real (supersedes the points in section 6 for the shift build):

- **Money** is the shift's take, at menu prices:
  - **Prices:** savory 6 for $12.50, 10 for $20.80; gooey butter 1 for $3.65, 3 for $10.50. A box that goes out earns its item price. When the whole ticket is out, the customer tips on the ticket total: more for fast, more for a clean streak, half for anything sloppy.
  - **Lost product is Matt's money.** Every rav on the floor, in the oil, in the trash (a box sent back), picked out cooked, or given away (strays and extras) costs what the customer would have paid for it: a beef is about $2.08, a gooey $3.65. Matt notices.
- **Reputation** is the restaurant's stars, and it's the fail state. Start at 4.
  - Fast clean tickets raise it. Slow tickets, strays, greasy or uneven ravs, boxes sent back, wrong orders and expired tickets lower it.
  - At 1 star Matt sends you home.
- **Clock-out:** what you made Matt, your best shift, Matt's money on the floor, and the stars you left him.
- **Next:** presentation (the plating patterns, wax paper, sauce, dress) feeds both. A beautiful box tips better and earns reputation. That's the expo microgame.

All numbers in `src/config/money.ts`.

## 13g. Plating: pour, salt, box (Dan, 2026-11-06)

- **Timing is forgiving.** A few seconds past the timer is fine; on the line the window runs about 15 real seconds. Past it the ravs **burst** (filling out the seam), they don't burn. Overdone ravs just get darker brown, never gray or black. Every flavor fails by bursting.
- **Pour, don't toss.** Drop a drained basket on the salt bowl and you're at the pass. Bring the basket over the bowl (one thumb) and it dumps on its own, near corner first; the other thumb can hold the bowl and meet it. Two hands, one motion, like the line.
- **Salt and toss.** Drag the shaker over the bowl and shake it (down-up strokes, or taps). Grab the bowl by its lip and slide it side to side to toss. Both together salt the bowl; the bowl's tag counts it down.
- **Box them two ways.** Hold the bowl over a box and it tips toward it and pours, a rav at a time, so you can pull away on the count. Or pick ravs out one at a time. Same verb as the basket: bring a container over its target and it pours. Both are allowed on purpose: it's a physics toy and players should find their own way.
- **The pass coaches you.** One line at the bottom always says the next step (dump, salt, toss, box, serve), and the thing you need next breathes a little. Until the bowl is salted and tossed, touching it grabs the bowl; after that, touching a rav picks the rav.
- **Dessert skips the salt.** Gooey butter goes from the basket straight into its box with tongs (tap and drag one at a time). Salted dessert is a complaint.
- **The metal table** holds the boxes, the prepped ones from expo plus a plain box for whatever you're plating if you didn't prep one. Swipe a box up by its wall, or hit SERVE on its tag.
- Salt is a presentation point (with size, wax paper, label, and sauce).
