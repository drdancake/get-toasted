# T-rav game: research notes (2026-11-03)

## Nour: Play With Your Food (TJ Hughes / Terrifying Jellyfish, St. Louis; Panic; Unity + Blender)
- Experimental "food art game": no scores, goals, timers. ~20 vignette dishes unlocked linearly; spawn endless ingredients, use tools (knife, tenderizer, blowtorch, dye) and spells (recolor, resize, freeze, dance, fire); photo mode. 30-120 min playthrough.
- Music is the interface: each food announces itself with a sound then acts as percussion; action frequency = "measure of chaos" shaping the song; no rhythm grid; rhythmic play charges spells; note sequences trigger effects; button mashing = Warp Mode. Score by Maximilian C. Mueller + James Morrison (Fluke Nukes). IGF 2024 Excellence in Audio nominee.
- DualSense: adaptive triggers/haptics for slicing; mic: blow to cool/scatter, slurp, sing a root note to levitate, yell to charge.
- Look: rigid-body physics tuned by trial and error (not soft-body); shader library (SSS noodles, depth-blended milk tea); textures from real meal photos under neutral light; Mirror's Edge lighting; sliders "past 10"; pop-art/food-blog saturation; Ghibli hunger; "visual ASMR". Gorgeous at distance, textures soften up close.
- Dev: 2017 Twitter studies -> Kickstarter ~$29k -> PS5 reveal 2020 -> shipped Sept 2023 ($14.99), Switch 2024 ($9.99). ~6 years.
- Reception: OpenCritic 65. Loved color, tactility, haptics, music. Criticized: over in an hour, no reason to return, no tutorial, awkward physics.
- Hughes quotes: "play is universal, food is universal"; "a playground where you can play with your food and no one will yell at you"; good physics games allow both tidy arrangement and chaos. Mueller: "the best interfaces don't have to be learned." Hughes next interest: physical/third-place projects.

## Toasted ravioli
- Origin: The Hill, 1940s. Oldani's (now Mama's on the Hill): cook Fritz, tipsy on scaloppini wine, dropped ravioli in the fryer; Mrs. Oldani dusted with Parmesan, sent to the bar; Mickey Garagiola "bet my house on it"; 1943 Star-Times ad is the earliest proof; Lou Oldani coined "toasted" to avoid "fat fried". Rival: Angelo's/Charlie Gitto's (1947, German cook misheard "drop some raviolis"). Lombardo's claims a 1930s Sicilian family recipe. Running gag: it's fried, "toasted" is marketing.
- Process: roast beef+veal (or pork) with mirepoix, grind fine with spinach, bind with egg + Parmesan -> thin egg dough -> fill, fold, press out air, mark with ravioli pin, cut square -> FREEZE (key; stops bursting, makes breading stick) -> egg+milk wash -> Italian seasoned breadcrumbs pressed firm -> refreeze -> deep-fry 350-375F, 2-3 min from frozen -> drain -> toss hot with Parmesan (+parsley) -> warm marinara.
- Failure modes: oil too cool = greasy/soggy; too hot = dark crust cold center; unfrozen = breading slides off, seams burst, filling leaks; air/overfill/wet filling = bursts; crowding = pale and limp; cold = leathery. Louisa box: "if they begin to puff, remove immediately."
- Culture: bar food, appetizer, party trays, Christmas; Busch Stadium (Taste of the Hill burger comes with two; 2026 stand sells them outright); Louisa Foods (Baldetti family, Jennings MO, national frozen) insists they didn't invent it. Siblings: Provel/Imo's, gooey butter cake, Ted Drewes, pork steaks. Locals half-defend, half-apologize. RFT: "What happened to good toasted ravioli?" (frozen replacing handmade).
- Visual: ~2 in square pillow, slight dome, fluted/ridged border, sometimes fork-crimped (Lombardo's: irregular half-moons; Gitto's: compact no frame). Colors: pale cream -> beige wet crumb -> amber gold at 2 min -> deep brown/black edges if overdone. Crust matte, pebbly, sandy, herb flecks, white Parmesan dust in the texture. Bite: audible crunch, crumbs flake, thin chewy pasta layer, filling grey-brown crumbly (meat) or molten white stringy (cheese), puff of steam; breaks along the seam; cross-section crumb/pasta/filling. Marinara thick, glossy red smear, drips at corner. Plating: red plastic basket or paper boat with wax paper (bar/stadium); white oval plate stacked 6-8 with ramekin (restaurant).
